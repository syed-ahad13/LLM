## Q1. What did the merges learn?

Of the first 50 merges, 23 rebuild a two-byte letter from its bytes (ا at 1, ی at 2, ک at 4, ر at 6, و at 7, ے at 8, ...). 4 produce a token that is not valid UTF-8 on its own: 2 are a space plus the lead byte of the next letter (␣\xd8 at 3, ␣\xd9 at 5), the byte-level start of a word, and 2 end in the middle of a character (ا\xd8 at 12, ␣ا\xd8 at 35). Across the merge list, letters fall from 70% of merges 1-10 to 0% of merges 10,001-49,743, while words rise from 0% to 71%: bytes, then letters, then words.

The 20 longest tokens (25-29 bytes, e.g. ␣انسائیکلوپیڈیا) are all whole words, none junk. ## Q2. How does it compare with existing tokenizers?

<!-- BEGIN generated:q2 -->
On test.txt (1,520,007 words) our tokenizer needs 1.19 tokens per word, against 5.99 for GPT-2, 1.63 for o200k_base, 1.36 for xlm-roberta-base; it splits 3.0% of words into 3 or more tokens, GPT-2 97.4%. GPT-2's byte-level vocabulary was learned on English web text, so an Urdu letter (2 bytes) often costs a token of its own (1.40 bytes per token against our 7.04), while o200k_base, with four times our vocabulary spread over many languages, sits in between.

Ours is the most efficient on 5 of 8 inputs (test.txt, News, Poetry, Diacritics, Names); it loses on Urdu + English (1.32 against 1.12 for xlm-roberta-base), Roman Urdu (2.47 against 1.51 for xlm-roberta-base), Numbers, dates (1.52 against 1.38 for xlm-roberta-base); question 3 looks at why.

Tables: `report/tables/compare.tex` (tokens per word), `report/tables/compare_metrics.tex` (all metrics, test.txt). Figure: `report/figures/tokens_per_word.pdf`.

## Q3. Three inputs our tokenizer handles badly

<!-- BEGIN generated:q3 -->
Ranked by our tokens per word and by our-to-o200k_base ratio (sum of the two ranks), the worst inputs are Roman Urdu (2.47; 1.58x o200k), Diacritics (2.73; 0.77x o200k), Numbers, dates (1.52; 0.77x o200k), Urdu + English (1.32; 1.03x o200k). The three chosen: Roman Urdu, Diacritics, Numbers, dates.

**Case 1: Roman Urdu.** Roman Urdu costs 2.47 tokens per word against 1.56 for o200k_base (ratio 1.58; 42.4% of its words take 3 or more of our tokens). Only 1.06% of the pre-token occurrences in train.txt contain a Latin letter, so our merges cover few Latin-script words: announcement → ␣ | ann | oun | c | ement (5 tokens, 0 times in train.txt); samjhaya → ␣s | am | j | h | aya (5 tokens, 0 times in train.txt); isliye → ␣is | l | iy | e (4 tokens, 0 times in train.txt). o200k_base learned Latin script from English and many other languages; it needs 1.56 tokens per word here and 1.63 on test.txt.

Excerpt (12 words; ours 25 tokens, o200k_base 16, GPT-2 19): A | aj | ␣sub | ah | ␣se | ␣b | ar | ish | ␣h | o | ␣ra | hi | ␣h | ai | ␣a | ur | ␣s | ab | ␣l | og | ␣ | gh | ar | ␣m | ein

**Case 2: Diacritics.** Diacritics costs 2.73 tokens per word against 3.53 for o200k_base (ratio 0.77; 47.4% of its words take 3 or more of our tokens). We still beat o200k_base here, but it is among our costliest inputs. Written Urdu usually omits diacritics: only 0.64% of the pre-token occurrences in train.txt carry one. Each mark is a separate 2-byte character between two letters, so the merges learned for the bare word no longer apply: تَشَکُّر → ␣تَ | ش | َک | ُّ | ر (5 tokens, 0 times in train.txt); اَچّھا → ␣اَ | چ | ّ | ھا (4 tokens, 0 times in train.txt); اِنتِظار → ␣اِنت | ِ | ظ | ار (4 tokens, 0 times in train.txt); against the bare تشکر → ␣تشکر (1); the bare اچھا → ␣اچھا (1); the bare انتظار → ␣انتظار (1).

Excerpt (9 words; ours 14 tokens, o200k_base 17, GPT-2 47): ل | َب | ␣پہ | ␣آتی | ␣ہے | ␣دُعا | ␣ب | َن | ␣کے | ␣ت | َم | َن | ّا | ␣میری

**Case 3: Numbers, dates.** Numbers, dates costs 1.52 tokens per word against 1.96 for o200k_base (ratio 0.77; 12.9% of its words take 3 or more of our tokens). We still beat o200k_base here, but it is among our costliest inputs. The pre-tokenizer cuts digit runs into groups of at most three and makes every separator its own pre-token, so long numbers, phone numbers and ID numbers fall apart: 35202-1234567-1 → ␣352 | 02 | - | 123 | 456 | 7 | - | 1 (8 tokens, 0 times in train.txt); 1,273,551,819 → ␣1 | , | 273 | , | 551 | , | 819 (7 tokens, 0 times in train.txt); 0800-12345 → ␣0 | 80 | 0 | - | 123 | 45 (6 tokens, 0 times in train.txt). o200k_base, which has the same three-digit rule, does worse; xlm-roberta-base does better (1.38 tokens per word).

Excerpt (12 words; ours 15 tokens, o200k_base 21, GPT-2 49): اجلاس | ␣14 | ␣اگست | ␣202 | 5 | ␣کو | ␣صبح | ␣10 | : | 30 | ␣بجے | ␣شروع | ␣ہوا | ␣اور | ␣شام

**Normalization.** The same 26-word sentence with Arabic kaf, yeh and heh in place of the Urdu letters (22 characters differ) costs us 73 tokens raw and 27 normalized; o200k_base 44 and 30, GPT-2 126 and 145. The Arabic forms occur in 0 of the 170,041,947 pre-token occurrences in train.txt, because normalize() mapped them away before training, so every word containing one falls back to short pieces. That is why the cleaning step normalizes, and why text must be normalized the same way before encoding. GPT-2 is the exception, cheaper on the raw sentence, presumably because the Arabic letters are more common than their Urdu forms in its training text.
